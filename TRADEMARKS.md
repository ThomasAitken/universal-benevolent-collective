# Trademark and official-identity policy

Status: interim founding policy, pending review by an appropriately qualified
Australian intellectual-property lawyer and any future UBC legal entity.

## Purpose

The Universal Benevolent Collective software is open source. Open-source
permission to use, copy, modify, and distribute the software does not grant a
right to misrepresent the origin, endorsement, constitutional status, or
official standing of a modified project.

This policy covers, to the extent protected by applicable law:

- the name **Universal Benevolent Collective**;
- **UBC** when used to identify this project, its software, or its services;
- current and future UBC logos and distinctive project branding; and
- terms or badges that claim **official**, **certified**,
  **constitution-conforming**, or equivalent UBC status.

No registered-trademark status is claimed by this document. Do not use the
registered trademark symbol (`®`) unless and until the relevant mark has been
registered in the applicable jurisdiction.

## Uses that do not require permission

Subject to applicable law, permission is not required to:

- refer truthfully to the Universal Benevolent Collective project;
- state that software is derived from, compatible with, or a fork of UBC,
  provided the statement is accurate and does not imply endorsement;
- link to the official repository or quote project material as permitted by
  its applicable licence; or
- retain factual copyright, licence, provenance, and attribution notices.

A fork should use a clearly different primary name and branding. References to
UBC should be descriptive and no more prominent than reasonably necessary to
explain the fork's origin or compatibility.

## Uses that require written permission

Written permission is required to:

- use a covered mark as the primary name or branding of another product,
  service, organisation, hosted network, domain, package, or account;
- use a project logo or a confusingly similar logo;
- describe a release, service, node, or network as official, certified,
  endorsed, or constitution-conforming; or
- use a covered mark in a way likely to cause confusion about source,
  sponsorship, governance, or affiliation.

## Constitution and official status

The software licence permits forks, including forks that remove constitutional
features. A fork that removes, replaces, or ceases to enforce the applicable
constitution must not present itself as an official UBC implementation or
network.

Official status is determined by project governance and technical provenance,
including the applicable constitution version and digest, signed release
metadata, and current admission and compatibility policies. Passing a
technical compatibility test alone does not grant trademark permission or
official status.

## No effect on software freedoms

This policy governs project identity, branding, and representations of
affiliation. It does not restrict the permissions granted by the software
licence, or lawful uses of a mark that do not require the owner's permission.

Questions and permission requests should be directed to the repository owner
through the project's official repository contact channels until a dedicated
contact or legal entity is established.
